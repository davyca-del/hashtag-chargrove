Chargrove could be built on. Your councillors vote this month.

Between 1,500 and 4,000 homes have been discussed for the fields around Chargrove Lane. Whether that land goes into the Strategic and Local Plan is decided in October. Writing to your councillors now is the part that matters most.

13 October Cabinet considers the plan 19 October Full Council votes

What to do

  1. Email your councillors before 13 October

    Cabinet is where the recommendation is settled, so aim for the 13th rather than the 19th. Names and addresses are below.

  2. Write it in your own words

    Two or three paragraphs is plenty. Identical copied letters get counted as a single response, so please don't forward a template — say what this place means to you and why you think it is the wrong site.

  3. Include your full address and postcode

    It shows you are a constituent, and MPs' offices require it before they will take up a case.

  4. Ask for a reply

    Ask them to tell you their position on including Chargrove before the vote. A written answer on the record is worth far more than a nod at the door.

Points you might make

  • The evidence is fifteen years oldThe Green Belt assessment behind the plan dates from 2011 and the landscape work from 2013. The Green Belt Review promised for August 2026 still has not been published. Councillors are being asked to release Green Belt land before the evidence meant to inform that decision exists.
  • This is the narrowest point of the gapIt is the only Green Belt keeping Cheltenham and Gloucester apart, and Up Hatherley Way is the last defensible edge. Once building crosses it, nothing obvious stops the next application.
  • The roads and services are already fullThe A46 Shurdington Road is at or beyond capacity at peak times and cannot be widened. The schools, surgery and shops built for the last expansion are at capacity too.
  • The Chargrove Triangle floodsIt is the parcel in this area most at risk of flooding, and parts of Warden Hill already flood. Surfacing land that currently holds water moves the risk onto existing homes.
  • There are alternativesSites to the north and north-west of Cheltenham could meet the borough's requirement several times over, where the gap between the two towns is far wider.

Pick the one or two that matter most to you. A short letter about the flooding on your own street carries more weight than a long one covering everything.

Two things that don't count

A comment on Facebook is not an objection. The councils have confirmed that social media responses will not be accepted as formal representations. It has to be an email or a letter.

A copied template is not an objection either. Identical text from many people is recorded as one response. Your own words, however plainly put, count for far more.

Then again in November

If the plan is approved in October it goes out for a six-week public consultation, known as Regulation 19, from late October or early November. That is the stage that reaches the independent Planning Inspector, and the councils have confirmed in public that every response will be passed to them.

Anyone who objects at that stage also earns the right to speak at the examination hearings in 2027. So please write in October, and then write again when the consultation opens. We will let you know the moment it does.
